## Why this experiment exists

The goal is to use multiple live submission slots as a controlled ablation study rather than as five nearby parameter guesses.

All five candidates inherit the exact V32 runtime package and change only a sparse learning/arbitration layer.

The experiment asks which *kind* of learning-from-loss mechanism, if any, transfers to the live competition population.

## Expected interpretation

### If N1 improves

This would support the strongest conservative thesis: a champion can be improved through very small, causally verified residuals without broad adaptation.

### If N3 improves more than N1

The important signal is likely not the intervention itself but **when the policy allows itself to change**.

### If N2 improves more than N3

Episodic similarity may preserve important local structure that the generalized regime model averages away.

### If N4 improves more than N2 and N3

Fast episodic retrieval and slow statistical generalization may provide complementary information.

### If N5 wins

The integrated architecture has evidence that opponent forecasting, regime inference, episodic memory, and causal residuals can be combined productively.

### If N5 underperforms simpler agents

Complexity is not free. Remove whichever contextual layer fails to add policy value.

## What not to conclude from an early rating

Do not treat one early rating snapshot as a final ranking.

The useful evidence is the combination of:

- episode count;
- rating trajectory;
- opponent strength;
- seat;
- residual activation;
- actual replays;
- whether losses are known-hard or surprising.

The purpose of the live experiment is to acquire external evidence, not to tune thresholds directly against the ladder.

## Packaging validation

The five artifacts were built from the exact V32 archive and separately repacked.

Sandbox checks performed before upload:

- exact V32 SHA verified;
- each tar contains root `main.py`;
- each exact final tar was extracted and imported independently;
- 1,438 sequential synthetic calls were exercised across both seats per variant;
- market actions remained within the 10-order limit;
- observed behavioral differences were sparse and occurred only at intended intervention points.

The official `kaggle_environments` package was unavailable in the local sandbox, so the hosted Kaggle environment remains the final runtime check for these experimental wrappers.
